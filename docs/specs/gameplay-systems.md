# Gameplay Systems Specification (V1 focus)

Status: **Draft from product discovery deep-dives (chat history entries 9 and 10). Pending owner review.**
Scope reference: [product-vision.md](product-vision.md). All numeric values below are placeholders to be tuned: every parameter must be data-driven and easily adjustable (see "Tuning principle").

## Tuning principle

All balance values (cargo prices, production rates, vehicle stats, town thresholds, interest rates, achievement goals, contract goals) live in data files, not code. Adding a cargo, industry, vehicle, or need must be possible by adding data only. Content must be easily expandable well beyond the V1 catalog.

## 1. Cargo

- Two classes in V1: **passengers** and **cargo**. Cargo types may be mixed in one vehicle in V1.
- V2: liquid class, and cargo that cannot be mixed.
- Freight cargo has an explicit **manufacturing level** 1–3 (matching its producer industry chain level); passengers use manufacturing level 0. Town needs draw from these levels.
- Cargo has a **fixed price** per unit. Higher-level products are worth more than lower-level ones, and some chains are more valuable than others.
- **Distance pay** (Issue [#73](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/73)): a delivery pays price × units × distance ÷ reference distance, where the distance is the Manhattan distance in tiles between the station where the vehicle's current load started (the first unit loaded into the empty vehicle) and the delivery station, at least the minimum distance (`deliveryPay` in `economy.json`: price paid at 30 tiles, minimum 4). It applies to freight, passengers and mail; contract bonuses keep the flat price. The fraction of a money unit is carried on the vehicle to its next delivery, so pay does not depend on how unloading is split across ticks. A load whose origin is unknown (saves before format 24) pays the minimum distance.
- Towns have a random supply and demand bonus applied to the price.
- V2: transit-time penalty, so that long routes are unprofitable with slow early vehicles.

## 2. Industries and production chains

- V1 catalog: about **5 chains of 3 levels, so about 15 industries** (e.g. wood, planks, furniture; wheat, flour, bread).
- Levels: level 1 = primary (raw), level 2 = secondary, level 3 = final.
- Recipes are **fixed with strict ratios** (e.g. 2 planks + 1 metal = 1 furniture).
- **Primary industry growth** (Issue [#73](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/73)): a level 1 industry that ships at least 60 % of its monthly production for 12 months in a row gains a production level, up to level 6; each level adds 25 % of the base output per pulse (rounded down), so a forest goes from 4 to 10 units per pulse. A poorly served month resets the streak; production never shrinks. The finished month is evaluated on the first tick of the next month, before loading or production resets the month stats. Processing industries still produce only from delivered input. Values in `industryGrowth` in `economy.json`; level and streak are saved from format 24.
- Level 3 products have no purpose except satisfying town needs.
- The industry map is **fixed during a game in V1** (generated at start, seeded). Starting maps place every chain (Issue [#193](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/193)): 3 / 2 / 1 instances of each level 1 / 2 / 3 industry on Small, 6 / 4 / 2 on Medium, and 12 / 8 / 4 on Large (`worldgen.json`).
- **Industry stock** (Issue [#193](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/193)): an industry stores only its **output**, and only the output counts toward its maximum stock. Primary industries produce on a fixed pulse. A processing industry turns delivered inputs into output **at once**, one recipe per complete set of inputs; output above the maximum stock is lost. Input units that do not yet complete a recipe wait in a **hidden leftover** that is not shown and does not count toward the stock, capped at one recipe's worth per input (the rest is lost). A delivery to an industry whose stock is full is still accepted and paid. Cargo that is not one of the industry's inputs is not unloaded and stays on the vehicle.
- V2: random spawning, buying industries (expensive), industries closing with a delayed warning, production upgrades bought with money, production decline when unserved.
- **Player-built factories are V2** (decided in deep-dive C, superseding the earlier V1 intent). In V1 the player builds stations and roads/rails only; the V1 late-game money sink is vehicle unlocks and expansion.

## 3. Stations

- Separate **road stations** and **rail stations** (Issue #55 locks the V1 placement and connection rules below).
- V1: **fixed station size** (1×1 tile); V2: several sizes to accommodate more vehicles.
- V1: **no catchment area**. A station must be **orthogonally adjacent to an industry or to any town building** to load/unload there. A catchment radius may be reconsidered in V2.
- The station tile **is** the connection point for its mode (road graph node or rail graph node). Matching road or rail attaches on an **adjacent** tile, not on the station tile itself.
- **Build does not require** adjacent matching road/rail; the player may place the station first and connect later. Isolated stations are valid but unreachable until connected.
- **Station tile occupancy (both modes):** the tile must be empty of object, road, rail, road station, and rail station. Road and rail may still share non-station tiles with each other.
- **Town and industry footprint:** each town and each industry occupies a 2x2 block (4 tiles; Issue [#219](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/219)). No road, rail, or station can be built on any of the four tiles, and a station loads or unloads at a site when it is orthogonally adjacent to any of them. Sites keep a two-tile gap, so one tile never touches two sites. Saves from before the footprint tag only the anchor tile; on load the other three tiles are tagged when free (no save format change).
- **Multimodal hubs:** a road station and a rail station may sit on different tiles that are both orthogonally adjacent to the same town or industry.
- Road/rail connection building must be **very simple** in V1.
- Stations are **stops only** (Issue #132): they hold **no cargo inventory**. Vehicles load from / unload into the unique orthogonally adjacent industry (or town freight is consumed on unload). Map generation keeps industry/town sites at least Chebyshev distance 3 apart so one station cannot attach to two sites.

## 4. Vehicles and routes

- **Orders/routes** between stations. A new route starts at its first stop; a vehicle standing on one of the route's stations starts at that stop instead, so the vehicle loads there before its first departure (Issue [#193](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/193)).
- **Capacity** is mandatory: it impacts load/unload time and travel costs.
- Loading: by default **load what is available and leave**; an option to **wait for full load** is included in V1 if not too complex.
- **Trains (V1):** buying a train purchases the **locomotive only**. The player adds and sells **wagons** separately. Cargo capacity is `wagonCount × capacityPerWagon` (placeholder: 10 per wagon). The locomotive's maximum wagon count is a model property (placeholder fixed max of 3 until the vehicle catalog). A locomotive with **zero wagons** has capacity 0 and **stays parked** even if it has a route. Selling a wagon while over the new capacity **discards** excess cargo. Map placeholders show the locomotive plus one marker per wagon.
- Road vehicles keep a single placeholder capacity until the shared vehicle catalog lands.
- Roughly **10 vehicle types** at V1 with increasing capacity and/or speed and different costs. The **data-driven road and rail vehicle catalog** is Epic [#11](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/11) / Issue [#145](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/145) (M5), not M3.
- Unlocking: vehicles **appear by year** and are then **unlocked with money** (Issue [#63](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/63)). Each vehicle type has its own unlock cost and the unlock is **permanent** (persisted in save format 19+). Core rejects buy/unlock when the calendar year or unlock state fails. Newer types are incremental improvements (e.g. a truck with a little more cargo, a train with a little more speed), and each one carries more per dollar of price and upkeep than the type before it, so upgrading pays off. This is a major progression upgrade, only affordable after saving for it. Timeline (Issue [#73](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/73)): trucks in 1904, 1910, 1918 and 1926, locomotives in 1907, 1914 and 1922, with unlock costs from $250,000 to $4,000,000; see [economy-progression.md](economy-progression.md).
- V2: depots, servicing/maintenance, aging.

## 5. Economy and finance

- V1 costs: **fixed per-vehicle upkeep per calendar month** and **vehicle purchase price** (data-driven in `vehicles.json`). Buy rejects when cash is insufficient. Sell credits **half** the current purchase price (a train refunds half the locomotive plus half a wagon × wagon count). Parked vehicles still pay upkeep. If monthly upkeep exceeds cash, cash **clamps at 0**. **Bankruptcy occurs when cash reaches 0 and ends active gameplay with a simple game-over screen** (Issue [#62](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/62)). A route can lose money if unprofitable.
- V2 costs: cost per distance travelled, infrastructure upkeep, and loans with interest until repaid. Loan terms and interest are to be tuned with the whole economy. Roads, rails, and stations stay free in V1.
- No inflation, no subsidies.
- **Finance ledger (V1)** (Issue [#66](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/66)): Core records every company cash change under a category, for the current and the previous calendar year. Income: deliveries (road and rail, all cargos), contract bonuses, and vehicle sales (vehicles and wagons). Spending: running costs (the amount actually charged after clamping at zero cash), vehicle purchases (vehicles and wagons), and vehicle unlocks. The category is recorded in the same place as the cash change, so over any period the change in cash equals total income minus total spending. On the first tick of a new calendar year the current year becomes "previous" and the current year resets to zero. The ledger is saved from format version 22 (older saves start empty). Out of scope for V1: monthly breakdowns, charts, and per-vehicle finance beyond the existing return on investment.
- **Score (V1)** = company cash + resale value of every owned vehicle (Issue [#64](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/64)). A vehicle's resale value is exactly what selling it refunds at current catalog prices (half the truck price; half the locomotive plus half a wagon × wagon count), whatever its state (moving, parked, loading, no orders). Selling a vehicle or a wagon therefore leaves the score unchanged, and buying one lowers it by the non-refundable half. Excluded in V1: roads, rails, and stations (free); money spent on vehicle unlocks (permanent and not sellable, so an unlock lowers the score by its cost); cargo on board, towns, and industries. The score is computed on demand and adds no saved or hashed simulation state. MapPlay shows it live next to the cash in the side panel; the finance recap and game-over display come with Issue [#66](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/66).

## 6. Towns

- Everything is highly tunable via `towns.json` (starting population range, max population, growth tiers). Placeholder numbers: towns start at about 100 to 500 inhabitants and can grow to about 10,000.
- Towns start with 3 level 1 needs; the tiers add a level 2 need at 800 inhabitants, a fourth level 1 need at 1,500, a second level 2 need at 2,200, a fifth level 1 and the level 3 need at 3,000, and a third level 2 need at 5,000 (Issue [#73](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/73)): 5 level 1, 3 level 2 and 1 level 3 needs from 5,000 inhabitants.
- Needs are assigned deterministically per town from freight cargo of the matching manufacturing level (stable prefix when population grows into a higher tier). Tiers never ask for more needs of a level than there are cargo types of that level (5 level 1, 3 level 2 and 1 level 3 at the maximum population); content loading rejects tiers that do.
- **Need plans** (Issue [#193](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/193)): each town's full ordered need list per level (its needs at the maximum population) is fixed when the town is created and saved; the current needs are the prefix the population tier unlocks, so later needs stay hidden until then. Map generation deals the plans from one seeded deck per level so every level 1, 2 and 3 cargo is planned by at least `minTownsPerNeededCargo` towns (3 in `towns.json`), and raises the configured town count to the minimum that allows it (15 with the V1 catalog: 5 level 3 cargo types, one level 3 need per town).
- **Town names** (Issue [#193](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/193)): each town gets a unique real-world capital name, picked from the seed out of the `names` list in `towns.json` and saved with the town. Content loading rejects a list shorter than the largest town count.
- Each need has flat monthly **demand** (`demandPerNeedPerMonth` in `towns.json`) and tracks **supply** (units delivered that month). A need is met when supply ≥ demand; the **growth score** is the count of met needs. At month end the town gains `populationPerSatisfiedNeed` inhabitants per met need (clamped to max population), which can unlock more complex needs. Town unload accepts only needed freight. Vehicles load a cargo only if some stop on their route accepts it (town need or industry input).
- Population growth increases **passenger demand**. Passengers and mail travel **between towns only** in V1 (no internal town transport). Towns periodically generate passenger-class cargo (`passengers` and `mail`) into a capped waiting inventory (`passengersPerPopulationUnit`, `mailPerPopulationUnit`, `passengerWaitingCapacity` in `towns.json`); every populated town generates at least 1 passenger and 1 mail per pulse. Vehicles load from the town bag at adjacent stations when the route visits at least two distinct towns, and earn the distance pay of their `pricePerUnit` delivering to a different town (origin tracked on the vehicle; same-town unload is refused). Passenger delivery does not credit freight needs.
- V2: shrink penalties for unmet needs.

## 7. Goals: achievements and contracts

- Sandbox with score.
- **Achievements** (formerly "main quests"; tiered tracks since Issue [#223](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/223)): each achievement is a track that measures one value and has three milestones, bronze, silver, and gold, each with its own catchy title (for example Log Haul, Timber Run, Wood Magnate for wood sold).
  - Defined in `achievements.json` (data-driven): a `goalScalePercent` per map size (Small 50, Medium 100, Large 200) and the tracks in panel order. Each track has an id, a `kind`, a `labelKey` (every kind except `cargoSold`, which shows the cargo name and icon), a `cargo` for `cargoSold` only, an optional `scalesWithMapSize` flag, and exactly three `milestones` (a positive `goal` and a title `nameKey`) with strictly increasing goals. Kinds: `achievementsUnlocked` (the global track: the percentage of the other tracks' milestones unlocked, rounded up; at most one track, goals 1–100, never scaled), `lifetimeIncome` (delivery income plus contract bonuses over the whole game; start cash and vehicle sales do not count), `contractsCompleted`, `vehicleTypesUnlocked` (paid model unlocks), `roadVehicleCount` and `trainCount` (owned at the same time; wagons do not count), `roadTilesTravelled` and `railTilesTravelled` (counted when a vehicle finishes a road or rail edge), `largestTown` (largest town population), and `cargoSold` (units of one cargo delivered and paid to any destination, passengers and mail included). A scaling track's goals are multiplied by the map size percentage with integer math (rounded down, at least 1). Bad data (duplicate ids, a goal of zero or less, goals not strictly increasing, not three milestones, a missing or forbidden label or cargo, an unknown kind or cargo id, a second global track, a missing or non-positive goal scale) is rejected at load.
  - Checked every simulation tick after town growth, vehicle upkeep, and contracts; the global track is checked last so it counts the milestones completed in the same tick. Each milestone completes on its own, with the id `{track id}.{tier}` (for example `sold_wood.silver`). Completion is **permanent** (it stays even if the value later drops, such as after selling trucks), records the completion date, **pays nothing**, and **does not end the game**. One completion event per milestone is published on the Core event bus ([ADR 0024](../adr/0024-core-event-delivery-contract.md)).
  - The lifetime delivery counters per (cargo, destination kind) increase only when a road or rail delivery is accepted and paid; they are saved from format version 20. Lifetime income, contracts fulfilled, and road and rail tiles travelled are saved from format version 25; older saves start them at zero. Completed ids no longer present in content (such as the V1 flat achievements) are kept and ignored, and the global track counts only milestones of the current catalog.
  - Shipped set (first pass, calibration pending): the global track at 20 / 50 / 100 %, lifetime income 1M / 10M / 100M, contracts 5 / 25 / 100, vehicle models unlocked 2 / 4 / 7, trucks 50 / 150 / 400, trains 15 / 50 / 150, road tiles 10k / 100k / 1M, rail tiles 5k / 50k / 500k, largest town 1k / 5k / 10k, and one cargo-sold track per cargo (raw goods 2k / 10k / 100k, processed goods 1k / 5k / 50k, finished goods 500 / 2.5k / 25k, passengers 4k / 20k / 200k, mail 1k / 5k / 50k). Every track except the global, vehicle models, and largest town ones scales with the map size; the goals above are the Medium values. Pacing target: about 20 % after an hour of play, 50 % after a few hours, 100 % only with a nearly maximized map and a renewed fleet.
  - The achievements window (key H, or its button at the right of the top bar just before Save / Load) is a near full-screen window with a close cross (also Escape or H) over a dimmed backdrop; the game pauses while it is open and resumes at its previous speed when it closes. It shows the Achievements, Company goals, and Resources delivered sections; each track shows its label, current value, and a bar filled in blue up to the value, with the bronze, silver, and gold trophies evenly spaced along it (the fill is linear between trophies) and each trophy's title, goal for this map, and completion date. Unlocked trophies are in full color; locked ones are greyed out. Progress comes from a read-only, on-demand Core query per kind.
- **Contracts** (formerly "random quests"): a bonus way to earn money. Each contract is "bring N units of product X to industry or town Y"; only X delivered to Y counts. Time-limited, possible town growth bonus, **no penalty on failure**.
  - Tuned in `contracts.json` (data-driven): maximum open contracts, amount range, duration in months, reward multiplier, and town population bonus (placeholders: at most 3 open, 50–200 units, 12 months, multiplier 5.0 since Issue #73, +100 inhabitants). Bad data (a negative maximum, a minimum below 1 or above the maximum, a duration below 1 month, a negative multiplier or bonus) is rejected at load.
  - Contracts are active as soon as they appear (there is no accept or decline command) and are freight only; passengers and mail are never offered. Core tops up open contracts to the maximum on the first simulation tick of a game (or after a load) and at each new month. Each new contract picks, with equal odds, an eligible target and cargo: a town and one of the freight goods it currently needs, or an industry and one of its input cargos. Two open contracts never share the same (cargo, target); with fewer eligible targets than free slots, fewer contracts are created. The amount is rolled in the data range from a dedicated saved random generator seeded from the world seed, the deadline is the start date plus the configured months, and ids are world-unique and sequential.
  - A road or rail delivery counts toward an open contract only when it is of that cargo, to that target, and accepted and paid (the same rule as the achievement delivery counters). Each tick, after town growth and vehicle upkeep and before achievements, Core completes contracts, expires overdue ones, and tops up. Reaching the amount completes the contract: it pays a one-off bonus of amount × cargo price per unit × reward multiplier (rounded down) on top of normal delivery income, and a town contract also adds the population bonus to that town (capped at the maximum population) through the normal population path, so needs and route cargo filters refresh. Later deliveries are paid as normal deliveries only. A contract still open after its deadline expires: it is removed and its progress is lost, with no penalty.
  - Offered, completed, and expired events are published on the Core event bus ([ADR 0024](../adr/0024-core-event-delivery-contract.md)) for the contracts UI ([#66](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/66)). Open contracts, the next id, and the generator state are saved from format version 21; a saved contract whose cargo or target is no longer valid for the loaded content is dropped.
  - The contracts panel (Issue [#66](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/66)) lists the open contracts with cargo, target (town or industry), delivered / required, deadline, and the bonus (the same Core formula as the payout), or an empty state when none is open. Clicking a row selects the target tile and centers the camera on it without leaving the panel.
  - Transient toasts announce an achievement milestone unlocked (the tier's trophy icon, its tier and title, for example "Bronze achievement unlocked: Log Haul") and a contract offered, completed, and expired, driven by the Core events. A toast never pauses or blocks the game, hides after a placeholder number of real-time seconds, and the stack has a small visible limit (the oldest toast is dropped first).
  - Out of scope for V1: distance limits on targets, passenger and mail contracts, and failure penalties.

## 8. Calendar

- Starts on **January 1, 1900**. The simulation uses a **fixed 365-day year with no leap days**. One in-game year is **30 real minutes at normal speed**, derived from the configured simulation tick duration; the displayed date advances by whole days and retains sub-day progress. Pausing advances no ticks and therefore no date; speed multipliers advance the calendar through additional simulation ticks. Vehicle availability is tied to the year, then unlocked with money. The 30-minute pacing remains subject to later playtesting and tuning.

## 9. Controls and UI (V1)

- Mouse and keyboard only. Basic camera: pan in 4 directions, zoom in/out. No minimap in V1 (deferred to V2; see closed issue [#39](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/39)).
- MapPlay displays the current calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` format and provides X1, X2, and X3 simulation speed controls. X1 is selected by default; the date remains derived from simulation ticks and follows the existing pause and speed behavior.
- Screens: a **top bar** with cash, score, date, speed controls, and the button of every list and goal panel; a **build toolbar** with one Delete tool (removes the station on a tile, otherwise its road and rail); **vehicle list** with return on investment (ROI), which shows every vehicle and offers no buying or route editing while no station is selected; with a station selected it lists only the vehicles of that station's kind that are parked or routed through the station, and offers buying that kind; **industry list** with production and transported amounts; **town list** with needs supplied versus demand plus passenger/mail Generated/Transported; **finance recap** (key F: this year and last year by category with total income, total spending, and net result); **achievements** (key H: a near full-screen window that pauses the game, see section 7); **contracts** (key K). The finance, contracts, and the older list panels share one screen area, so opening one closes the others and the achievements window. The game-over screen shows the final score and the bankruptcy date. Town, industry, and contract list rows link to the map (select tile, center camera); selecting a town or industry tile opens the matching list, highlights the row, and scrolls the list so that row is the top one. Modern, clean style.
- **Map labels** (Issue [#193](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/193)): each industry shows `Name ([input icons] => [output icon]) | stock [output icon]`, for example "Sawmill ([wood] => [planks]) | 10 [planks]", and a primary industry `Name | stock [output icon]`. Each town shows `Name ([icons of its current needs]) | passengers [passenger icon] mail [mail icon]` with the passengers and mail waiting there. Each truck and train shows the amount and icon of every cargo it carries, for example "40 [wood]". The icons are the UI cargo icons baked into map sprites. Labels separate the name part from the stock with a pipe (`|`), never a dash, so a count never reads as negative. The towns list shows the town name.
- **New game screen** (Issue [#67](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/67)): MapPlay opens on a full-screen new game panel (same scene, no separate menu scene); no world, map, or simulation exists until Start is pressed. The seed field starts with a random seed and accepts whole numbers from 0 to 18446744073709551615 only; the Random button rolls a new one (UI-side randomness, never a Core generator), and an invalid or empty seed shows an error and disables Start. Three toggles pick the map size: Small 128×128 (default), Medium 256×256, Large 512×512. The map, camera, and shortcuts ignore input while the screen is open. During play the side panel shows the seed and size next to the score (for example "Seed 123456 · 256×256"); the game-over screen shows them with the final score and date and offers a **New game** button that reloads the scene back to the new game screen. Continue and Load game (below) load a saved game from this screen.
- **Save and load** (Issue [#183](https://github.com/sekoya-solutions/sekoya-transit/issues/183)): a **Save / Load** button in the top bar opens a panel with the autosave and five numbered manual slots. Each row shows the in-game date, seed and map size of the save (or Empty). **Save** on a manual slot overwrites it; the autosave slot only offers Load. The game autosaves into the autosave slot on the first day of each in-game month without pausing the simulation; a failed autosave is reported in the panel and never interrupts play. **Load** replaces the running game; because unsaved progress is lost, the panel first asks "Unsaved progress will be lost. Load anyway?". A damaged save or one written by a newer version shows an error and leaves the running game untouched. On the new game screen **Continue** loads the most recent save (the slot written or loaded last, else the autosave) and **Load game** opens the same slot list; both are disabled while no save exists.

## 10. Save and audio

- One autosave slot plus five manual slots (see the save and load bullet in the new game section). The initial versioned save snapshot includes the world seed, simulation tick, generated map channels, and ordered town and industry placements; the calendar date is derived from the tick. Later gameplay state is added through explicit save-format migrations. WebGL persistence to be investigated later. No map sharing.
- Audio: sound effects (UI click and map selection, build, destroy, cash in on a paid delivery) plus **one looping ambient music track** in V1, from owner-selected CC0 assets listed in [CREDITS.md](../../CREDITS.md) ([ADR 0018](../adr/0018-audio-approach.md)). Music volume, effects volume and mute are set in the **Sound** panel (speaker button in the top bar), apply at once, and are per-player settings, not saved in the game file. Music starts with the game, so it already plays on the new game screen once the browser allows audio (after the first click on WebGL). The cash-in sound plays at most once per minimum gap of real time (a scene value of `MapPlayAudio`, default 0.5 s), so fast-speed deliveries do not stack.

## 11. Map grid

- Maps are square: **128-by-128**, **256-by-256**, or **512-by-512** tiles, chosen by the player on the new game screen together with the seed. V1 terrain is flat. The number of towns and industries, their spacing (at least 10 tiles between any two sites, diagonals included, and at least one free tile between a site and the map border), and the producer distance of consumers are data per size in `worldgen.json` ([content-format.md](content-format.md)); larger maps get more towns and industries. Generation raises the town count to the need-plan minimum (section 6) when the configured count is lower. A new game is a seed plus a map size; the same pair always builds an identical world. The save format is unchanged: the seed is already saved and the size follows from the saved map channels.
- Core uses zero-based `(x, y)` grid coordinates and separate flat arrays for terrain type IDs, object/entity IDs, and ownership IDs. The row-major tile ID is `y * width + x`; isometric projection stays in presentation.
- Terrain type ID `0` is the default flat terrain. Object ID and owner ID `-1` mean no object and unowned, respectively; non-negative IDs, including `0`, are valid.
- V1 Core generation composes flat terrain with seeded town and industry placement. Town object IDs follow town placement order; industry IDs follow all town IDs in industry placement order. Each placement routine uses its own fixed random stream, and owner IDs remain unassigned.
